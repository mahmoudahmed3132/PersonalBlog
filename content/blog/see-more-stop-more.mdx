In July I spoke at CrowdStrike's Adversary Simulation event. The talk made a single argument: **when you buy a security product, make it prove itself on your own environment, through an adversary's eyes.** This post is the long-form version.

## The bet you're making

When you buy security, you're buying a promise. The vendor's slide says it blocks "100% of known and unknown threats." The average breach costs **$4.44M** (IBM, Cost of a Data Breach 2025) if that promise turns out to be wrong. And the risk, the downtime and the customer notification are yours.

The vendor gets paid whether or not the product stops the breach. That asymmetry is why the burden of proof belongs on your side of the table.

## The old proof is obsolete

Vendor demos were built for an adversary that dropped a file and waited for it to be scanned. That adversary is mostly gone:

- **29 minutes** average eCrime breakout time. The fastest recorded was 27 seconds (CrowdStrike 2026 Global Threat Report).
- **82%** of detections are malware-free. There's no file to scan.
- **84%** of severe breaches used living-off-the-land tradecraft, and **71%** abused PowerShell, which is already trusted on your hosts (Bitdefender LOTL telemetry, 2025).

Modern intrusions abuse the tools you already trust. A canned demo never shows you that.

## Offense runs in minutes, response takes weeks

Put the numbers side by side. Breakout takes **29 minutes**. Median dwell time is **14 days** (Mandiant M-Trends 2026). Identifying and containing a breach takes **241 days** on average (IBM 2025).

Detection alone can't close that gap. An alert that lands in a queue is working on your SOC's clock, not the attacker's. Only prevention that acts on its own operates on the adversary's clock.

That's also the business case. Speed of containment is the biggest cost lever you control: organizations with automation and autonomous response saved **$1.9M** per breach and identified and contained it **80 days** faster. So the question to ask a vendor isn't "does it alert?" It's "**does it act, on its own, before the objective completes?**"

## Why the datasheet can't answer that

- **100%** is a lab number: known samples, default tuning, no evasion.
- **Alert ≠ block.** Detection gets quietly counted as protection.
- **It's not you.** Their lab isn't your estate. Your legacy apps, your exclusions and your blind spots never show up in it.

The only question that matters is: _will it stop this, here, on my environment?_

## See more vs. stop more

These are two different values, and most evaluations blur them together.

**See More (detection).** An alert is raised, but nothing is blocked. The objective completes anyway, and containment depends on how fast your SOC is. You're buying forensics.

**Stop More (prevention).** The action is enforced: the process is killed, the request blocked, access denied. The objective fails, and it's contained regardless of response time. You're buying outcomes on the adversary's clock.

A product can win every detection metric and still lose every breach. Score both, separately.

## The POC problem

Most proofs of concept can't answer the question either. They're usually:

- **Steered.** The vendor runs it, picks the scenarios and avoids its weak spots.
- **Narrow.** It tests the console, the integrations and the agent's footprint, not whether the product stops a breach.
- **Scripted.** Known samples with no evasion and no LOTL. It's closer to a guided demo.
- **Unscored.** There's no verdict per technique, so you can't compare vendors fairly.

A POV the vendor runs is built to prove value, not to expose failure.

## Adversary emulation: not a scan, not a pen test

- A **vulnerability scan** answers "what weaknesses exist?" You get a list of CVEs. It says nothing about whether your control would catch the exploitation.
- A **penetration test** answers "can a skilled human get in?" It's one path, run once, and depends on the tester. You can't compare vendors with it.
- **Adversary emulation** runs the same catalogue of techniques identically against every product and scores each technique. Because it's repeatable, it's comparable. Because it's comparable, it's a buying tool.

## The method: same rig, every vendor

The only variable should be the product.

1. **Build the range from your gold image.** Your OS, apps and policies, cloned once per vendor.
2. **Map real TTPs to ATT&CK.** From recon to impact, including evasion.
3. **Isolate each phase** so every verdict is clean and attributable to one technique.
4. **Record each disposition with evidence:** command lines, artifacts, timestamps.
5. **Re-test after tuning** to prove the difference.

My reference run covered **12 techniques** across **8 ATT&CK tactics** and produced **426 sensor events**. It's small enough to run per vendor and wide enough to cover the kill chain.

## Three buckets

Every technique ends up in exactly one bucket:

- **Prevented (best).** Enforced, the objective fails, and no human was needed.
- **Detected (conditional).** Alerted but not blocked. Containment depends on response time.
- **Silent (worst).** No alert and no artifact. It's invisible to detection _and_ to forensics.

**Silent is worse than Detected.** A detection leaves a trail you can investigate. Silent leaves nothing.

## Trap 1: the prevention illusion

In one run, a single PowerShell phase produced **32 alerts**. It looked bulletproof. The five pre-impact techniques around it (recon, privilege escalation, credential access, collection and C2) produced **0 events**.

Alert volume isn't coverage. **Score by position in the kill chain, not by alert count.** One silent recon gap outranks a hundred execution alerts.

## Trap 2: partial prevention is failure

**14 of 28** LSASS dump attempts were blocked. The other 14 completed, including all 12 critical events.

Credential access is binary. One hash dump owns the domain, and stolen credentials show up in **32%** of breaches and **22%** of initial access (Verizon DBIR 2025). Fifty percent blocked means breached. Make binary objectives like this a **gate**, not a score.

## Three postures

A real test usually shows one of three postures. These are anonymized archetypes, not brands, and the numbers below are illustrative, not an endorsement:

- **The Blocker (Vendor X).** Prevention-led with aggressive blocking. Thinner visibility, and harder to tune.
- **The Watcher (Vendor Y).** Deep telemetry and detection, but lighter on autonomous enforcement.
- **Balanced (Vendor Z).** Moderate on both, and strongest against novel tradecraft.

## Buy area, not a spike

Plot each vendor on four axes: prevention, detection, novel tradecraft and false-positive control. X spikes on prevention (85) but collapses on novel tradecraft (41). Y spikes on detection (95) but collapses on FP control (40). Z has no collapsed axis: 72, 83, 82 and 74. It covers the most area.

A tall spike with a collapsed axis is a single point of failure. Buy area.

## The best tester isn't the best buy

Add cost and operational load and the ranking shifts again. The top performer often sits in "premium": the best efficacy, with diminishing returns for the money. The best buy is usually the product with near-leader efficacy at the lowest cost and load. And the one that looks cheapest on the quote is often the most expensive to run.

## Your playbook

Six steps before you sign:

1. **Threat-model your business.** Pick the adversaries and TTPs that target your sector.
2. **Build a range from your gold image,** cloned per vendor.
3. **Emulate the full chain,** including the LOTL tradecraft that demos skip.
4. **Classify every technique** as Prevented, Detected or Silent, with evidence.
5. **Score with weights and gates.** Rank on your risk, and eliminate anything that fails a gate.
6. **Make vendors close the gaps, then re-test.** That turns the POC into a recurring control.

The tooling is all open: MITRE ATT&CK, Atomic Red Team, Caldera, Sysmon and your SIEM.

## Five things to remember

1. **Test on your turf.** Emulation on your estate measures reality.
2. **Seeing isn't stopping.** Score detection and prevention separately.
3. **Weight the chain, gate the binaries.** Credential integrity is pass or fail.
4. **Demand the denominator.** "Coverage percent, out of what?"
5. **Tools are necessary, not sufficient.** The answer is a program: the right product, tuned with your engineering, and re-tested every renewal.

Don't buy security blind. The right control is the one whose measured, tuned coverage survives a real adversary on your estate.
